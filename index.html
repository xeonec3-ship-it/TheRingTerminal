Exhibition: "Unfinished Nature"

Curated by Maestro Mao · School of Pointillism

The Ring does not hide its workshop — it exhibits it as part of the piece. Guests are not led to finished canvases, but to ones still being written, because, by Maestro Mao's teaching, a work completed while its subject still lives is not art — only a sketch.

Every exhibit in this catalogue is signed not with a name but a rank: the Student shapes the form, the Docent chooses the colour, and the Maestro decides when a work is finished. The Maestro's judgment is not open for discussion — not by the subject, and not by the room.

Exhibit I
"Skin, Stretched Without a Frame"
Pointillism in its purest form: a thousand punctures in place of dots of paint. Attributed to the School of Pointillism, rank of Student. The curator notes that the subject remained conscious longer than the concept required.
Exhibit II
"Portrait in Cross-Section, Side View"
A work of the School of Fauvism: the animal mask on the subject was meant to remain unremoved for the duration of the sitting. Maestro Mao judged its premature removal a breach of protocol — the exhibit was completed without it.

The Ring

One of the Five Fingers of the City's Backstreets

The Ring holds enough influence in the City to rival the Wings themselves, yet its business is neither trade nor violence — it is art. Formal members are artisans devoted to depicting the human condition, and they pursue that devotion by causing the suffering they depict, then exhibiting it. The organization is built the way a school is built: around teaching, apprenticeship, and the passing of technique from master to student.

Student
Shapes the raw form of a work. Lowest standing in the hierarchy.
Docent
Oversees auctions and the movement of finished pieces.
Maestro
Holds the galleries and has final say over when a work is complete.

The Ring is not one workshop but several, each organized as a School devoted to a different artistic discipline. The Schools argue constantly over whose direction is truest to the craft — but the disagreement is a family one: every member accepts that a Maestro's work carries real depth, whichever School trained them, and pieces are often produced jointly across Schools.

School of Pointillism
Founded in the discipline of dots
School of Fauvism
Wild colour, unconstrained hand
Named for the "wild beasts" of the old world's own Fauvist movement, this School favours strong colour and unrestrained brushwork over careful representation. Its artisans are recognisable by the animal masks they wear during a sitting, worn as both signature and instrument — tradition holds that the mask should not be removed before the work is finished, whatever the sitter has to say about it.
School of Cubism
Every angle, one gaze
The Cubists pursue a single ambition: to hold three-dimensionality within one unbroken gaze, the way the old-world movement tried to show every facet of a subject at once, on one flat plane. In the Ring's hands this becomes a literal problem of composition — a single work assembled from a subject seen, and taken apart, from every angle at once.
School of Corporism
The body as material, not subject
The most literal of the Schools: where the others depict the body, the Corporists compose with it directly — flesh, bone, and tendon reassembled and reanimated as both the artwork and, when required, its own instrument. Corporist pieces rarely stay still in the gallery the way the other Schools' work does.

Aside from the ring-shaped insignia every member wears to mark rank, Docents and Maestros are known by their white avant-garde suits — a V-cut collar, buttoned near the sternum. It is, in its way, the only uniform courtesy the Ring extends to anyone: you will know a Maestro before the sitting begins.

Unlisted · School of Suprematism

Zero of Form

Suprematism seeks the zero of form, to ascend past imitation towards pure artistic feeling through simply arranged geometry upon a stark, white canvas. To reach nothingness is excellence. To be pure is to be void.
