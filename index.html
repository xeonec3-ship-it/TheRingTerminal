Invitation to the Gallery

The door holds no lock — only memory. The curator who summoned you gave two words: a rank, and a name. Speak them both, and the doors will know you.
The uninvited are exhibited too — they are simply never asked for consent.

Exhibition: "Unfinished Nature"

Curated by Maestro Mao · School of Pointillism

The Ring does not hide its workshop — it exhibits it as part of the piece. Guests are not led to finished canvases, but to ones still being written, because, by Maestro Mao's teaching, a work completed while its subject still lives is not art — only a sketch.

Every exhibit in this catalogue is signed not with a name but a rank: the Student shapes the form, the Docent chooses the colour, and the Maestro decides when a work is finished. The Maestro's judgment is not open for discussion — not by the subject, and not by the room.

Exhibit I
"Skin, Stretched Without a Frame"
Pointillism in its purest form: a thousand punctures in place of dots of paint. Attributed to the School of Pointillism, rank of Student. The curator notes the piece was meant to strip the subject down to zero before completion, and that they remained conscious far longer than the concept required.
Exhibit II
"Portrait in Cross-Section, Side View"
A work of the School of Fauvism: the animal mask on the subject was meant to remain unremoved for the duration of the sitting, to preserve the pure line of the concept. Maestro Mao judged its premature removal a breach of protocol — the exhibit was completed without it.

Guests are reminded: leaving the gallery is possible at any point during the exhibition. Becoming part of the next catalogue is not.

Notable Members

A partial record — the Ring keeps no complete registry

Most of the Ring's Students go undocumented outside their own School. The names below surface because their work, or their conduct, made them impossible for the rest of the City to ignore.

Jumsoon Maestro
Formerly of the School of Pointillism
A Maestro of the School of Pointillism, known for an androgynous appearance, a veiled white gown, and spot-tattoos spreading across chest and face. Jumsoon ran the School's gallery as both auction house and laboratory, most notoriously pursuing refraction experiments through the so-called Glass Window — work that consumed several kidnapped subjects and drew the attention of outside Fixers. Armed in the field with a red multi-bladed spear, Jumsoon proved as dangerous in person as in reputation. The gallery, and the Maestro's experiments with it, did not survive the scrutiny they attracted.
Status: deceased
Ina Maestro
School of Cubism · one of the Ring's founding Maestros
One of the Ring's original founding Maestros, and currently the one holding the Cubist seat. Unpredictable even by the Ring's standards, Ina is spoken of as something closer to pseudo-immortal than mortal — those who claim to have crossed paths with her more than once insist she never quite looks the same twice, as if she simply wakes up in another body come Sunday. Her name also surfaces wherever the kaleidoscope drug and the Cube are mentioned, though nobody in the gallery will say exactly how the three connect. Beyond that, nothing further is recorded here.
Callisto Maestro
Formerly of the School of Corporism · called "Nursefather"
Once the Corporist wing's Maestro, Callisto broke apart his own organic body and rebuilt it as full-body prosthetics, treating the procedure as the first stroke of his life's work rather than its cost. His magnum opus, a blade called Tibia, is composed from what remained of his original corpus — ten years in the making, and gouged badly enough in a single afternoon to set that work back by all of them. He no longer holds the Maestro's title within Corporism, but the Ring still answers to him under another name: Nursefather.
Albina Apprentice
School of Corporism · Callisto's apprentice
Taken on by Callisto after his previous apprentice left the House of Spiders, Albina has adopted his philosophy in full — that the body is not the artist's tool but its first material. She shadows her Nursefather closely, and those who have watched her work say she shows every sign of intending to surpass him at it.
Éponine Docent
School of Corporism
A Docent of the Corporist notion, inducted into the ranks after turning her unrequited love, Marius, into a sanguine axe. She now hunts the one he used to chase, in an attempt to reunite the lovers in eternal matrimony.
Bella Trial Docent
School of Corporism · one of the last, since their Maestro's departure
One of the very few Corporists still standing after their Maestro left the School behind. Bella is clever, composed, and strikingly beautiful — though that face is a mask, shaped after what she used to look like. Beneath it is a body that no longer resembles a human one at all.
Alex Trial Docent
School of Corporism · head of the Forensics Department
One of the few remaining Corporists who still knows basic anatomy with real precision, recently raised to Docent and put in charge of the Ring's Forensics Department. Alex teaches new Students the fundamentals of Corporism and anatomy from the ground up — and quietly cleans up after them whenever a lesson goes wrong.
Pablo Docent
School of Pointillism
Recognisable by the abstract mask fused from two human faces that she wears over half her own. A devoted attendant to Jumsoon's gallery, Pablo managed the Maestro's auctions and guest lists with the same care she gave the exhibits themselves — bandaged, composed, and rarely far from the centerpiece of the moment.
Pop Docent
School of Pointillism
A small, eccentric man layered in rings, drawn to what he calls "ecstasies" — refracted, kaleidoscopic states he chases through devices of his own design. Pop treats the gallery floor the way others treat a stage, and the line between his curatorial duties and his private fixations is not one he bothers to draw.
Asura Docent
School of Pointillism
Fiercely, almost devotionally loyal to Jumsoon and to the Ring itself, Asura swings between withdrawn stillness and open violence with little warning. She fights with a spiked steel pole, and those who have seen her mid-battle describe her hair lightening from black to white the longer the fight runs — as if the encounter itself were bleaching her.
Rufo Docent
School of Fauvism
Known through the Fauvist wing for her delicate hand at compositing animal skins — a skill she treats with the same composure she brings to everything else, right up until composure stops being useful. Rufo carries herself like someone who has never needed to raise her voice to be obeyed, and rarely has.
Mao Maestro
School of Pointillism · curator of the current exhibition
The Pointillists' current Maestro, and the one holding the gallery behind tonight's catalogue. Mao keeps no public dossier beyond what the exhibition itself discloses — those who ask after him directly are told, politely, to read the walls instead.
Kaito Trial Docent
School of Pointillism · assists with the gallery
Handles the parts of the exhibition Mao doesn't have patience for — the guest list, the hanging order, the quiet correction of a piece before the doors open. Kaito is rarely mentioned in the same breath as the Maestro's own work, which suits him: the gallery runs on time because he is the one keeping it.
Akiva Docent
School of Pointillism
One of the current Docents of Pointillism, hailing from the mountainous reaches of Y Corp territory. Widely held to have the finest eye for fashion of anyone in the Ring, Akiva keeps a standing fascination with Fauvism — particularly the avian end of it, and particularly when the sitting involves feathers rather than fur.
Shiranui Trial Docent
School of Pointillism · one of the Ring's Tailorists
A Trial Docent of the Pointillists, and one of the very few in the Ring dedicated to tailoring — responsible for the attire worn by fellow members, and reportedly overseeing where ████████ are used in place of fabric. Shiranui once belonged to the ████████, until witnessing what they considered an inadequate way of consuming that ingredient, one that left nothing behind but rags. Their past before that still remains unknown, for now.
The Cataloguer Unlisted
Compiler of this record
Every catalogue is assembled by someone the catalogue itself declines to name. Whoever set these pages — chose which exhibits to list, which words to gild, and which names to leave out entirely — goes unrecorded here, and has made no request to be found.

The Ring

One of the Five Fingers of the City's Backstreets

The Ring holds enough influence in the City to rival the Wings themselves, yet its business is neither trade nor violence — it is art. Formal members are artisans devoted to depicting the human condition, and they pursue that devotion by causing the suffering they depict, then exhibiting it. The organization is built the way a school is built: around teaching, apprenticeship, and the passing of technique from master to student.

Student
Shapes the raw form of a work. Lowest standing in the hierarchy.
Docent
Oversees auctions and the movement of finished pieces.
Maestro
Holds the galleries and has final say over when a work is complete.

The Ring is not one workshop but several, each organized as a School devoted to a different artistic discipline. The Schools argue constantly over whose direction is truest to the craft — but the disagreement is a family one: every member accepts that a Maestro's work carries real depth, whichever School trained them, and pieces are often produced jointly across Schools.

School of Pointillism
Founded in the discipline of dots
The Pointillists build their pieces from thousands of small, deliberate marks rather than broad strokes — a technique that, applied to a living canvas, becomes thousands of small, deliberate punctures. Their signature tool is a heavy brush built for the work, wielded like a spear when the sitting resists. The School currently answers to Maestro Mao, under whose hand the punctures have, if anything, grown more exact.
School of Fauvism
Wild colour, unconstrained hand
Named for the "wild beasts" of the old world's own Fauvist movement, this School favours strong colour and unrestrained brushwork over careful representation. Its artisans are recognisable by the animal masks they wear during a sitting, worn as both signature and instrument — tradition holds that the mask should not be removed before the work is finished, whatever the sitter has to say about it.
School of Cubism
Every angle, one gaze
The Cubists pursue a single ambition: to hold three-dimensionality within one unbroken gaze, the way the old-world movement tried to show every facet of a subject at once, on one flat plane. In the Ring's hands this becomes a literal problem of composition — a single work assembled from a subject seen, and taken apart, from every angle at once.
School of Corporism
The body as material, not subject
The most literal of the Schools: where the others depict the body, the Corporists compose with it directly — flesh, bone, and tendon reassembled and reanimated as both the artwork and, when required, its own instrument. Corporist pieces rarely stay still in the gallery the way the other Schools' work does.

Aside from the ring-shaped insignia every member wears to mark rank, Docents and Maestros are known by their white avant-garde suits — a V-cut collar, buttoned near the sternum. It is, in its way, the only uniform courtesy the Ring extends to anyone: you will know a Maestro before the sitting begins, though what waits at the center of the ring is, by design, a void.

Unlisted · School of Suprematism

Zero of Form

Suprematism seeks the zero of form, to ascend past imitation towards pure artistic feeling through simply arranged geometry upon a stark, white canvas. To reach nothingness is excellence. To be pure is to be void.
